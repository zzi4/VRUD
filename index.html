Manuscript Overview

Research inventory · 10 November 2026. The statistics below describe the three-site manuscript analysis. Dataset access is by application; the version and included resources supplied for a request are confirmed separately. This page does not distribute raw trajectories.

Automated driving in dense mixed traffic requires anticipating interactions between motor vehicles and heterogeneous vulnerable road users (VRUs) sharing limited road space. Uneven trajectory-data coverage of narrow streets and roadside activity restricts the development and evaluation of models for these environments. We present VRUD, a drone-based dataset collected at three urban mixed-traffic sites, comprising 8.50 hours, 30,554 retained trajectories, and seven road-user categories. VRUs constitute 74.40% of its retained trajectory inventory. Encounters are organized around a selected motor vehicle, preserving surrounding participants and their successive or concurrent behavior relations. Complementary spatial and temporal safety-surrogate measures characterize behavior- and participant-dependent interaction conditions. Forecasting experiments highlight the value of representative mixed-traffic training data and surrounding trajectories, while behavior-conditioned evaluation exposes differences hidden by aggregate errors. Together, the trajectory resource, interaction profiles, and forecasting benchmark support the study of heterogeneous mixed traffic and the selection of test conditions through behavioral coverage and safety criticality. VRUD can support research on interaction-aware prediction, cross-domain generalization, and scenario mining and generation for automated-driving evaluation. Dataset access requests: https://zzi4.github.io/VRUD/.

Collection Sites

VRUD covers a residential intersection (Site A), a residential street (Site B), and a mixed-use junction and adjoining streets with marked pedestrian crossings and roadside parking (Site C). Sites A and B are in Shenzhen, Guangdong, China; Site C is in Changchun, Jilin, China. Residential entrances, storefronts, and roadside activity create settings in which motor vehicles and VRUs share limited road space. All three sites use 4K resolution, an 80 m flight altitude, and a nominal frame rate of 30 fps.

Site A: residential intersection in Shenzhen, Guangdong, China. 41 recordings, 2.41 h.

Site B: residential street in Shenzhen, Guangdong, China. 23 recordings, 1.30 h.

Site C: mixed-use junction and adjoining streets in Changchun, Jilin, China. 87 recordings, 4.78 h.

Sites A and B include morning and evening acquisition periods. Site C covers morning and afternoon periods on October 13, 14, 16, and 17, 2026. All 87 available recordings from those four dates are included, bringing the three-site inventory to 151 recordings and 8.50 h. Registered OpenDRIVE maps connect the trajectories and road semantics at all three sites.

Behavior-Based Organization

Relative motion, the spatial ordering of oriented road-user boxes, and registered map semantics organize behavior intervals. Each scenario is centered on a selected car, truck, or bus ego and retains multiple surrounding participants and their successive or concurrent relations. Pairwise relations supply the basic units for behavior annotation and safety-surrogate analysis within that shared scene.

The shared vocabulary comprises 13 behavior types: following a leader, approaching a moving leader, approaching a stopped target, crossing, opposing approach, completed opposing passage, side neighbor, ego passing, ego being passed, corridor entry, corridor exit, route joining context, and route leaving context. Crossing has one behavior definition for pedestrians, bicycles, tricycles, and motorcycles; participant category is analyzed separately.

All 151 recordings have been processed. The primary sustained-motion cohort contains 14,105 directed-pair–behavior intervals involving 10,264 recording-local ego–VRU pairs. Both actors must each have at least one continuous second at recorded speed of 0.5 m/s or higher somewhere in their observed tracks; temporary stops remain. Intervals may overlap across behavior types and process views.

The scene index contains 5,513 selected-ego scenes, including 4,084 with multiple related targets over the scene and 3,032 with at least two targets interacting concurrently. Surrounding targets can include VRUs and other motor vehicles. These scene counts differ from directed-pair behavior counts.

Behavior-Conditioned Interaction Profiles

Observed box clearance, constant-velocity body-contact TTC, exposure, conflict-area PET, and applicable longitudinal measures describe complementary spatial and temporal characteristics. The default TTC horizon is 5 s; initial model overlap is kept separate from future model contact.

Seven behavior types with at least 200 primary intervals cover 13,717 of the 14,105 intervals (97.25%). Inventory Statistics

The three-site inventory contains 30,554 trajectories passing the rounded 2 m travel rule, including 22,733 VRUs (74.40%). Each retained trajectory has equal weight in the native track-speed average, including waiting. Trajectory Prediction and Scenario Diagnosis

A map-free Trajectron++ benchmark compares training on VRUD, SinD, INTERACTION, and joint-source data. Additional conditions use the focal trajectory alone, the nearest surrounding participant, or only Sites A and B for training. Seven training conditions use three random seeds (21 fitted models); the main cross-dataset comparison comprises 45 evaluation runs.

A common map-free Trajectron++ protocol uses 13,312 class-balanced training targets per condition and evaluates 1 s of observations followed by 3 s of prediction. On the held-out Site C date, the VRUD-trained model achieves class-macro minADE@6 of 0.367 ± 0.011 m, compared with 0.486 ± 0.002 m for SinD training and 0.721 ± 0.012 m for INTERACTION training. CAR and pooled pedestrian/bicycle targets receive equal weight in this macro average. Training on Sites A and B only gives 0.463 ± 0.008 m on the same test windows. Full neighboring context reduces CAR minADE@6 from 0.566 to 0.525 m. Behavior-linked analysis identifies 1,689 CAR windows; 1,603 have at least one quality-eligible detailed relation, while 1,536 satisfy the stricter pooled safety-surrogate checks. Pedestrian crossing contributes 627 clean windows across 25 recordings. These results describe one predictor and one held-out VRUD date; observed density and concurrent relations do not establish a monotonic context benefit. All ± values denote sample standard deviation across three training seeds.
